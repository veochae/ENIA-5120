# Final Project Rubric

**Total Points:** 100

## Project Overview

- **Team Size:** 2-3 students per team
- **Assignment Type:** Group final project
- **Deliverables:**
  - Presentation slides
  - Source file in `.qmd` or `.Rmd`
  - Knitted report in HTML or PDF

## Notes

- Students may choose their own topic and dataset.
- The knitted report should be presentable and professionally organized.
- Recommended section structure: Executive Summary, Data Source, Data Selection, Exploratory Data Analysis, Hypothesis Testing, Modeling, Modeling Results, Conclusion.
- Students may use LLMs and prior lab code, but the initial thinking and writing should be original.
- A post-course contribution survey will be used to identify low-contribution group members, who may receive a penalty.

## 1. Question and Storyline (15 points)
Frames a clear, meaningful question and builds a cohesive story around it rather than presenting disconnected analyses.

- **15**: Project has a strong central question, clear motivation, and a well-structured narrative that connects all parts of the analysis.
- **12**: Question and storyline are clear overall, with only minor gaps in focus or transitions.
- **8**: A reasonable question is present, but the report feels somewhat fragmented or uneven.
- **4**: Question is vague or weakly connected to the analysis.
- **0**: No clear question or coherent story is established.

## 2. Data Choice and Source Context (10 points)
Justifies the chosen data, explains why it is sufficient for the topic, and documents the source and relevant dataset context.

- **10**: Data choice is well justified; source, scope, variables, and relevance to the research question are clearly explained.
- **8**: Data choice and dataset context are mostly clear, with minor missing details.
- **5**: Basic dataset description is present, but justification or source/context is limited.
- **2**: Dataset is mentioned, but choice and relevance are weakly justified.
- **0**: Data source and rationale are missing or unclear.

## 3. Data Cleaning and Preparation (10 points)
Performs appropriate cleaning and preparation, including handling missing values, standardization, categorical alignment, and reporting dataset dimensions before and after cleaning.

- **10**: Preparation is careful and appropriate to the data; key cleaning decisions are evident; starting and ending dimensions are clearly reported.
- **8**: Cleaning is generally solid, with only minor omissions in documentation or preparation choices.
- **5**: Basic cleaning is present, but key preparation choices or dimension reporting are incomplete.
- **2**: Cleaning appears minimal, weak, or only partly appropriate.
- **0**: No meaningful preparation process is shown.

## 4. Exploratory Data Analysis (10 points)
Uses EDA to uncover relevant patterns tied to the topic at the variable and multivariable level.

- **10**: EDA is purposeful, topic-driven, and helps build the analytical case with relevant univariate and multivariable exploration.
- **8**: EDA is useful and mostly connected to the topic, with minor gaps in depth or focus.
- **5**: EDA is present but feels somewhat generic, limited, or only loosely tied to the topic.
- **2**: EDA is thin, poorly chosen, or weakly interpreted.
- **0**: No meaningful EDA is presented.

## 5. Hypothesis Testing (10 points)
Uses one or two appropriate hypothesis tests to evaluate claims or questions raised by the topic and data.

- **10**: Hypothesis tests are well chosen, correctly implemented, and clearly interpreted in context.
- **8**: Testing is solid overall, with only minor gaps in setup, explanation, or interpretation.
- **5**: A reasonable test is attempted, but motivation or interpretation is limited.
- **2**: Testing is weak, poorly matched to the question, or partially misinterpreted.
- **0**: No meaningful hypothesis testing is shown.

## 6. Modeling Approach and Tuning (15 points)
Chooses an appropriate modeling method, tunes or compares alternatives when relevant, and explains why the final model is the best choice.

- **15**: Model choice strongly fits the question; alternative models/settings are meaningfully compared; final model is well defended.
- **12**: Modeling workflow is strong, with only minor gaps in tuning, comparison, or justification.
- **8**: Model is reasonable, but the argument for why it is optimal is limited.
- **4**: Model choice or tuning process is weakly justified.
- **0**: No meaningful modeling workflow is shown.

## 7. Modeling Results and Interpretation (15 points)
Interprets model results correctly and clearly, including significant variables, effect direction, model evidence, and method-specific technical proof.

- **15**: Results are interpreted accurately and substantively; important variables or group characteristics are clearly explained; method-specific outputs are used well.
- **12**: Interpretation is strong overall, with minor gaps in precision, depth, or explanation.
- **8**: Main results are reported, but interpretation is somewhat mechanical or incomplete.
- **4**: Results are weakly interpreted or partly misunderstood.
- **0**: Model results are not meaningfully interpreted.

## 8. Visuals and Evidence Quality (8 points)
Uses visuals strategically to tell the story, prioritizing quality and relevance over volume, while still including technical plots when they are necessary.

- **8**: Visuals are polished, relevant, and integral to the story; technical plots are included when needed and interpreted effectively.
- **6**: Visuals are generally strong, with only minor issues in focus, quality, or interpretation.
- **4**: Some visuals are useful, but others feel generic, excessive, or weakly integrated.
- **2**: Visual evidence is limited, low quality, or poorly matched to the narrative.
- **0**: Visuals are missing or do not support the project meaningfully.

## 9. Conclusions and Synthesis (5 points)
Draws clear conclusions and explains how the evidence from cleaning, EDA, testing, and modeling supports or challenges the project's claims.

- **5**: Conclusion is clear, well synthesized, and directly tied to the project's evidence and claims.
- **4**: Conclusion is good overall, with minor gaps in synthesis or specificity.
- **3**: Conclusion is present but somewhat generic or only partly integrated with earlier sections.
- **1**: Conclusion is weak, vague, or disconnected from the analysis.
- **0**: No meaningful conclusion is provided.

## 10. Professionalism and Deliverables (2 points)
Report, source file, and slides are complete, presentable, professionally organized, and suitable as a future template for real-world analytical work.

- **2**: All deliverables are complete, polished, and professionally structured.
- **1**: Deliverables are mostly complete and understandable, but presentation or organization is uneven.
- **0**: Deliverables are incomplete, hard to follow, or not professionally presented.
